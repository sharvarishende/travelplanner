Sanjay Gandhi National Park

A Wild Forest at the Heart of a Megacity

About this Place

Why visit Sanjay

Sanjay Gandhi National Park (SGNP) is one of the most remarkable urban forests in the world — a 104 square kilometre protected area sitting entirely within the boundaries of Mumbai, one of the most densely populated cities on earth. The park is home to leopards, spotted deer, langur monkeys, wild boar, flamingos, and over 270 species of birds. It contains the ancient Kanheri Caves — a complex of 109 Buddhist rock-cut caves dating from the 1st century BCE — making it as culturally significant as it is ecologically extraordinary. On weekends, thousands of Mumbaikars escape here to cycle, trek, picnic, and simply breathe air untouched by the city's traffic.

History & Heritage

The story behind it

The forest has been protected since the 1940s and was declared a national park in 1983. The Kanheri Caves within its boundaries were continuously occupied by Buddhist monks from the 1st to the 9th centuries CE, making them one of the longest continuously used religious sites in Maharashtra. The caves contain elaborate viharas (residences), chaityas (prayer halls), and over 1400 inscriptions in Brahmi and Devanagari scripts.

Visitor Tips

Before you go

01

Enter early (7 AM) on weekdays to avoid weekend crowds and spot wildlife more easily.

02

The Kanheri Caves are a separate entry within the park — budget 2–3 hours to explore properly.

03

A toy train runs to Kanheri Caves from the main gate — good option if you don't want to walk.

04

Night safaris (pre-booked) offer a rare chance to spot leopards after dark.

05

Carry water, sunscreen, and insect repellent — the forest is humid and dense.
