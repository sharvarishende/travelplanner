Gateway of India

Mumbai's Most Iconic Landmark

About this Place

Why visit Gateway

The Gateway of India is the defining symbol of Mumbai — a grand basalt arch standing 26 metres tall on the waterfront of Apollo Bunder, overlooking the vast Arabian Sea. Built to commemorate the visit of King George V and Queen Mary to India in 1911, it was completed in 1924 in an Indo-Saracenic style that blends Hindu and Muslim architectural traditions with European sensibility. Today the Gateway is far more than a colonial monument — it is where Mumbaikars come at all hours to watch the sea, eat bhel puri, bargain for trinkets, and simply breathe. The majestic Taj Mahal Palace Hotel stands directly opposite, completing one of the most photographed streetscapes in Asia.

History & Heritage

The story behind it

The foundation stone was laid in 1911 but construction began only in 1915 due to World War I delays. Designed by architect George Wittet, the arch was the last site from which British troops departed India after Independence in 1948 — making it both a monument of colonial arrival and a symbol of its end. The adjoining harbour was historically used for the entry of viceroys and governors, and today launches depart from here for Elephanta Island, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Visitor Tips

Before you go

01

Visit at sunrise (6–7 AM) for golden light, no crowds, and stunning reflections on the sea.

02

The evening crowd is festive but very busy — ideal if you enjoy the energy of the city.

03

Take a harbour cruise from the jetty for the best views looking back at the Gateway.

04

The Taj Mahal Palace Hotel opposite offers high tea — a memorable splurge.

05

Watch out for persistent vendors and photographers — a polite firm 'no' works fine.
