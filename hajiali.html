Haji Ali Dargah

A Shrine Floating on the Arabian Sea

About this Place

Why visit Haji

The Haji Ali Dargah is one of Mumbai's most iconic and revered landmarks — a 15th-century mosque and tomb of the Sufi saint Haji Ali Shah Bukhari, situated on a tiny islet in the Arabian Sea about 500 metres from the Worli coast. It is connected to the mainland by a narrow 500-metre causeway that floods at high tide, creating the remarkable illusion that the white marble structure is floating on the water. The dargah is a place of profound harmony — visited equally by Muslims, Hindus, and people of all faiths who come to seek blessings, listen to qawwali music, and experience the extraordinary peace of the sea-surrounded shrine.

History & Heritage

The story behind it

Haji Ali Shah Bukhari was a wealthy Muslim merchant who renounced his worldly possessions and travelled across Central Asia and South Asia before settling in Bombay. According to legend, after his death his coffin miraculously floated to the islet where the dargah now stands. The present structure dates largely from the 15th century and was renovated extensively in the 20th century. The white Indo-Islamic architecture with its graceful minarets and central dome is considered one of the finest examples of Mughal-influenced architecture in Maharashtra.

Visitor Tips

Before you go

01

Check tide timings before visiting — the causeway is submerged at high tide and impassable.

02

Visit on Thursday or Friday evenings for live qawwali (devotional music) performances.

03

Dress modestly — cover your head and remove footwear before entering the inner sanctum.

04

The causeway walk itself (500 metres each way) is a meditative experience.

05

Arrive at sunset for the most dramatic lighting on the white marble structure.
